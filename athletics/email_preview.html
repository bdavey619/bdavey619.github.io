State of Play

Nick Kurtz's two-homer night put the A's in control early, and they never let Pittsburgh back in.

Kurtz finished the game 3-for-4 with five RBI, but the real story was when he hit them. Inning two, when the A's seized a lead they would expand to ten by the seventh. J.T. Ginn was clean through six innings on six hits and zero earned runs, and the bullpen behind him asked nothing of the offense after that. Justin Sterner and Mason Barnett combined for three scoreless frames with two strikeouts between them. This was Oakland's cleanest game in weeks: no drama, no collapse, no late-inning scramble. Kurtz just needed one swing per at-bat to matter, and he delivered both times before the outcome was ever in question. The offense converted five runners from scoring position across thirteen plate appearances. Not flawless execution, but enough when the pitcher never wavered. This is what a wire-to-wire win looks like without needing to survive anything.

What to Watch

Pittsburgh offered nothing after the first inning. Once Oakland led, the game was decided structurally. Pitching held, the lead expanded, and late runs only salted what was already secure. That's not sustainable against better lineups, but it's a reminder that this team can still dominate a full game when the starter eats innings and the bats show up early. Kurtz carried the offensive load, but it wasn't him alone; McNeil went 3-for-4 with a homer, and the lineup drew eight strikeouts without drowning in them. The issue is not whether they can do this. It's how often they will. They won the early battle and held it.
