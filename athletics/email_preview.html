State of Play

Ginn did everything right. They did almost nothing with it.

J.T. Ginn struck out ten and allowed two hits through eight innings. A shutdown performance that should have been enough. But the Athletics couldn't break through until the ninth, when Lawrence Butler singled to break a scoreless tie and take the lead. It lasted one inning. The Angels scored twice in the bottom half, and that was the game. The lineup left seven runners on base and converted zero of seven RISP situations. The critical damage came in the fourth inning, where they stranded three runners with one out. Ginn's dominance didn't matter because the bats never made it count until it was too late.

What to Watch

This team keeps finding moments and losing them. They had the chance to steal a game from a division rival on the road. A starter pitching a masterclass, a go-ahead run scored in the ninth. That should have been enough to hold. Instead, they learned again that one inning of pitching can erase eight innings of precision. The division doesn't forgive games like this. Neither does a team at 23-24 that has lost three straight and can't afford another one-run loss stacked on top of the last. This is the cost of offensive inconsistency: great pitching becomes ordinary when it doesn't have a safe margin.
