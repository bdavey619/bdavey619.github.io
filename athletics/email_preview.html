State of Play

Late-inning rallies are becoming the only way this team wins.

Kurtz walked it off in the tenth, but the game belonged to Wilson first. He drove in three runs across five at-bats to keep the offense relevant through six innings of damage. Down four after the Angels stormed ahead in the fourth, the Athletics clawed back in increments. Two in the seventh, two more in the eighth, then the tying run in the ninth. This is not how teams build leads. This is how they survive on thin margins. Springs lasted 3.2 innings and left the game in pieces. The bullpen. Sterner bleeding runs early, then Suarez, Barlow, and Alvarado locking it down. Bought just enough time for one more swing. Kurtz got it in the tenth. The pattern is clear now: the bats do not run. They wait. They convert runners on base into runs only when forced to, and the pitching has to hold long enough for that one moment to arrive. That only works until it doesn't.
