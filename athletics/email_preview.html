State of Play

Jacob Lopez turned six scoreless into a runaway by the third inning.

Henry Bolte supplied the lineup's power early, then the offense poured it on. Fourteen hits across a nine-run day against a Texas team that couldn't answer. The game broke open before the fourth was done, which meant the bullpen just had to breathe. Hartlieb and Harris combined for three scoreless innings and two strikeouts to close it out. This wasn't ugly. This was domination wearing a daytime uniform. Bolte's two home runs and four RBIs mattered because they converted early traffic into a margin the Rangers could not reverse. The Athletics wasted 12 runners-in-scoring-position situations but scored twice in them. Enough to matter when the team hit fourteen times overall. Lopez and two clean relievers made that work. The pattern of inconsistency held. They left runners stranded again, again, and again. But early power output buried the noise. One inning broke the game open, and the rest was follow-through. Nine-two isn't pretty. It's effective.
