State of Play

Jeffrey Springs left after 4.2 innings with 7 runs charged. The offense got theirs early, scoring twice in the first and again in the third, but Baltimore answered back with two momentum swings. Three runs in the fourth, then three more in the fifth. And the Athletics never caught up. Brian Serven hit a solo home run and drove in two runs, and the bullpen was sharp once the game got away: Blewett struck out four in 1.1 scoreless innings, Basso threw a clean inning, Morris gave up one run over two frames. But none of it mattered once the lead flipped in the fifth. This is a team that can't afford starters exiting after the fourth inning when the margin is this thin.

The gap to first place is now 16 games. They've lost three straight, which means the inconsistency that's defined the season just got louder. Serven produced. The lineup put nine hits on the board. But Jeffrey Springs couldn't hold it together in the middle innings and Baltimore's three-run fifth broke the game open. You can't win 5–8 when your starter gives up seven runs by the fifth inning no matter who shows up at the plate. The difference between a 53-win team and a contender is usually not firepower. It's starting pitching holding early leads. That's not a slump. That's a structural problem.

What to Watch

Jacob deGrom takes the mound tonight for Texas at 5:05 PT, carrying a 4.21 ERA into a matchup where the Athletics' current form offers little margin for error against the Rangers' rotation. This is where the next three games matter.
