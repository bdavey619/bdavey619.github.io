# Must Watch — Product Notes

Raw, informal. Not structured. Things worth remembering before they get lost.

---

- "this is the game worth fighting for on your calendar"
- ESPN shows you your team, not what matters
- don't miss moments > track every game
- storylines are the product
- attention allocation > content consumption
- the user's real question: "if I only get one night this week, when should it be?"
- the thing we're competing with isn't other sports apps — it's family dinner, sleep, a partner who wants to watch something else
- scores are outputs; stakes are the reason you watch
- a blowout with historic stakes is more must-watch than a close game that means nothing
- the product gives you confidence, not just information — "I did my research, this one is worth it"
- being out of the loop is the real failure mode — not low viewership, not casual fandom, but showing up Monday and having nothing to say
- the explanation is a social object — it's the thing the user repeats when they justify watching
- over-explaining why a game is good is not the same as explaining why it's worth someone's specific evening
- the list should feel like it was made by someone who has seen a lot of sports and knows what actually becomes a story
- narrative significance is retroactive — but you can see the setup from the outside in advance
- "I don't want to miss the game everyone will be talking about" — that's the fear we solve
- busy people don't need more sports coverage; they need a trusted editor
- the test: read the top pick out loud to someone. do they immediately understand why it matters? if not, keep rewriting.
- intraweek updates are only valuable if they change the answer — not just because something changed
