  • high Corn on the cob — the 24-hour sugar clock Late July — peak corn season Jul 26–Jul 27 · 2×

    Within 24 hours of harvest at room temperature, up to 50% of sweet corn's sugar converts to starch through enzymatic activity. This is why corn from a roadside farm stand tastes different from corn in a supermarket — not because of variety, but because of when it was picked and how far it traveled. Late July is peak sweet corn season across the US, and the ritual of eating it is inseparable from its biology.

    If the taste of sweet corn is largely determined in the 24 hours after picking, what are we eating when we buy it from a grocery chain — and why doesn't that matter more to us?

  • high Veraison — the week the grapes become smoke sponges Late July 2026 — pre-veraison window Bordeaux, France Jul 27

The moment veraison starts, grapes become acutely susceptible to wildfire smoke: volatile phenolic compounds in smoke bind with the sugars in the fruit during fermentation, creating flavors permanently described as 'ashy, smoky, or burnt' — unremovable even after harvest. Bordeaux vineyards are currently in the final days before veraison begins. The fire is 15 kilometers away. Winemakers cannot see from outside the vineyard whether the threshold has been crossed.
