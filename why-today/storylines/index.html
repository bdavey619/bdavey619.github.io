  • high Corn on the cob — the 24-hour sugar clock Late July — peak corn season Jul 26

    Within 24 hours of harvest at room temperature, up to 50% of sweet corn's sugar converts to starch through enzymatic activity. This is why corn from a roadside farm stand tastes different from corn in a supermarket — not because of variety, but because of when it was picked and how far it traveled. Late July is peak sweet corn season across the US, and the ritual of eating it is inseparable from its biology.

    If the taste of sweet corn is largely determined in the 24 hours after picking, what are we eating when we buy it from a grocery chain — and why doesn't that matter more to us?

Curiosities

  • high The sunset is still later than it was on the solstice Late July — the counterintuitive moment Jul 26

    The summer solstice was June 21 — the longest day of the year. But at most mid-northern latitudes, the latest sunset of the year doesn't happen on the solstice. It happens a week or two after, meaning right now — in late July — the sun is setting later than it did on June 21. The days are getting shorter from the top, with sunrise arriving earlier, but not yet from the bottom.

    If the summer solstice is the longest day of the year, why does the sun keep setting later for weeks after it — and what does that tell you about how the year is actually organized?

  • medium Why do all the stone fruits ripen at the same time? Late July — stone fruit peak Jul 26

    Peaches, nectarines, plums, apricots, and cherries all peak in the same six-to-eight-week window in midsummer — different species, different sizes, different colors — arriving together at farmers markets and vanishing together.
