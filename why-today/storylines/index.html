Natural World

  • The Rhine hit its lowest level since 1880 - and the cost is now quoted per tonne Aug 16–Aug 17 · 2×

    EUR 150/tonne vs EUR 20; 800t vs 5,100t past Kaub Rhine delta to lower Danube

    Kaub, the Rhine's shallowest chokepoint, fell below the 25cm record set in 2018 and to the lowest reading since records began in 1880. Barge operators say most traffic is now halted south of Mainz. The number that shows what a river is worth: a barge that normally hauls 5,100 tonnes past Kaub is moving about 800, and freight from Rotterdam to points south of the chokepoint has reached roughly EUR 150 a tonne against EUR 20 in normal water. Duisburg reports loading capacity at about a third of usual. Covestro has declared force majeure on polyether polyols at Dormagen, where propylene oxide can only arrive by ship. The Danube went the other way: it rose 19cm off its low, letting Hungary restart a Paks unit. Hungary's response to the reprieve was to start building a bed sill upstream of the plant - 145,000 cubic metres of rock, plus two 260-foot barges that may be sunk at the cooling pumps.

    When a river stops being reliable, the fix is now to re-engineer the river - what does it mean that this is cheaper than moving the thing that depends on it?

  • Lala never made landfall - and the lights may stay off for months Aug 16–Aug 17 · 2×

    ~200k without power; restoration in months Hawaii Island

    Lala reached Category 1, made its closest approach to the Big Island late Saturday, then weakened to a tropical storm and pulled away without an official landfall. It did not need one. The National Weather Service issued a flash flood emergency with rain falling at 3 to 5 inches an hour; two homes were swept away at Naalehu and one person is dead, with 100 or more homes on the southern Big Island destroyed or damaged. About 200,000 customers across Oahu, Maui and Hawaii island started Sunday without power. Hawaiian Electric has restored more than 63,000 and is flying in 100 mainland crew, but is telling the hardest-hit rural districts of Hawaii island to prepare for weeks - possibly months.

    Why is landfall still the headline metric for a storm when the damage is done by rain rate and terrain - and who is misled by that framing?

Human Rituals

  • Back-to-school - a record total, and everyone shopping at the same two places Jul 26–Aug 17 · 9×

    Walmart +22pp to ~80%; dollar stores enter top ten

    The NRF projects $146.8 billion in 2026 back-to-school spending, an all-time high, and JLL has parents budgeting $489 per child, up 11.7%. The composition is the story. Walmart's share of parents' shopping destinations climbed more than 22 percentage points in a year to nearly 80% - the highest any retailer has reached in the history of JLL's survey. Dollar stores entered the top ten for the first time, up about 10 points to nearly 32%. Mass merchandisers now reach more than 83% of back-to-school shoppers. The ritual is getting more expensive to perform and the places it is performed in are consolidating around price.

    What does it mean that the most reliable annual measure of household optimism is now rising for the same reason household strain is?

Places

  • Omis, Croatia - the town that had to be evacuated by boat Aug 17

    ~1,200 evacuated by land and sea; seven critical Omis, Dalmatia, Croatia

    The fire started Thursday evening at Lokva Rogoznica and covered the roughly 6km to Omis in a matter of hours, reaching the Borak neighbourhood above the town centre around 1:45am. Roughly 1,200 residents and tourists were moved out, about 1,000 of them into a sports hall. The part that describes the place: the evacuation ran by land and by sea, with harbour authority vessels lifting people out of stretches the road could not reach, and some people going into the water off boats to get away from the heat. Thirty-six were injured, fourteen hospitalised, seven critical. 286 firefighters, 90 vehicles and four aircraft worked it through the night; more than 1,000 hectares burned. It was the height of the Dalmatian season.

    What happens to a place whose geography is its whole economy when that same geography becomes the reason there is only one way out?

Curiosities

  • An invasive tree is now the pacing item on Hawaii's power restoration Aug 17

    42-mile transmission span under assessment Hamakua coast, Hawaii Island

    The reason parts of Hawaii island may wait months rather than days for power is not the storm and not the poles - it is a tree. Many of the island's transmission lines run through wooded ground where albizia, a fast-growing invasive introduced for reforestation, is tall enough and brittle enough to take a line out when it goes over. On the Hamakua coast crews are assessing a 42-mile span after fallen trees knocked down multiple poles and structures. The restoration schedule is being set by what grew alongside the corridor over decades, not by what the wind did in a night.

    How many of the systems we call infrastructure are actually being governed by something biological that nobody put on the asset register?

  • The 10-year is holding a 19-month high on a story nobody has printed yet Aug 8–Aug 17 · 6×

    10yr ~4.69%, near 19-month high of 4.75%

    The 10-year eased to about 4.69% on Monday, a basis point off the previous session, after rising roughly 5bp on Friday and testing a 19-month high of 4.75% earlier in the week. Over a month it is up 9bp; over a year, 35bp. The data that came in was benign - July CPI at 3.4% headline and 2.5% core, both in line, and flat producer prices. What is holding the long end up is not a print. It is the read that the Fed may be complacent about inflation risk while the Middle East keeps supply prices elevated.

    What is the 10-year actually pricing when it stays this high on data that keeps coming in exactly as expected?

  • The Hormuz premium stopped pricing a deal and started pricing a deficit Aug 8–Aug 17 · 6×

    Brent above $88; 8 transits vs ~130/day pre-war

    Brent held above $88 into Monday after a gain of more than 5% on the week, and oil edged higher again as traders looked for the next catalyst - renewed Israeli strikes on Lebanon and the prospect of fresh US sanctions on Iran. The transit number is the tell: eight vessels crossed the strait on a single recent day against roughly 130 a day before the war began on February 28. The IEA has cut its demand outlook, saying prolonged conflict and high prices are weighing on consumption, while expecting global supply to fall about 4% this year. The premium is no longer a bet on the negotiation; it is a bet on the supply gap outliving it.

    At what point does a war premium stop being a risk premium and simply become the price?

  • 170 days of war, a second country struck, and still no congressional vote Jul 22–Aug 17 · 19×

    day 170; Lebanon's deadliest day since June

    The war widened over the weekend without widening its authorisation. Israel's strikes in southern Lebanon killed 11 people on Sunday - the deadliest day there since the June ceasefire - including senior Hezbollah commander Abu Hassan Alaa; Netanyahu framed it as retaliation for a Hezbollah attack that wounded three soldiers on Saturday, and said Israel would strike again if threatened. Washington is preparing a fresh Iran sanctions package with the US-Iran ceasefire due to lapse. Iran's Supreme National Security Council has set out its conditions for reopening the strait: an end to US threats and attacks, lifting of the naval blockade, compensation, sanctions relief and release of frozen assets. Tehran says it is not negotiating with Washington at all, only with Oman, and only about a route. The war began February 28. The War Powers clock was first paused by a ceasefire and is now simply never started by a blockade.

    How does a country fight a 170-day war that closes the world's most important oil lane without Congress ever casting a vote - and what mechanism was supposed to prevent that?

  • The fire front reached the Adriatic - Landes, Carcassonne, central England, now Omis Jul 26–Aug 17 · 12×

    burned area 2.3x the 20-year average

    The season keeps finding geographies it was not named for. A fire out of Lokva Rogoznica ran into Omis on Croatia's Dalmatian coast, forcing about 1,200 residents and tourists out by land and sea, injuring 36 and leaving seven in critical condition; a woman's body was found in burnt scrub nearby on Aug 14. That followed a new fire through woodland in France's Landes that pushed more than 500 people out of Luglon, the Aude blaze fought to the edge of Carcassonne, and firefighters in central England working homes ablaze. European burned area hit 567,097 hectares as of Aug 12 against a 20-year average of 243,189 - about 2.3x normal - and at least 14 people have died in European fires this year.

    If the fire season is one continuous system moving across terrain rather than a sequence of separate fires, what decides where it goes next?

  • The tsunami warning cleared in hours; three days on, the toll is 53 and people still won't go home Aug 16–Aug 17 · 2×

    toll 53; 995 aftershocks; ~5,000 in shelters Flores, East Nusa Tenggara, Indonesia

    The M7.7 hit off Flores at dawn on Aug 15. Indonesia issued a tsunami warning and lifted it the same morning when monitoring showed no significant sea-level change. The toll went 2, then 20, then 38, then 47, then 51, and reached 53 on Sunday as rescuers recovered six more bodies. BMKG had logged 995 aftershocks by 3pm Sunday, only a few of them felt. More than 900 homes were destroyed and 450 damaged; about 5,000 people are in temporary shelters, and landslides are still blocking sections of the Trans-Flores Highway. Officials say some residents are staying in the evacuation sites not because their houses are gone but because of the aftershocks and the memory of the 1992 Flores quake and tsunami.

    Why does a tsunami warning resolve in hours while the death toll takes days - and what does that asymmetry say about which disasters we have learned to measure?

  • dormant Heat debt - when overnight lows refuse to drop, the body falls behind Jul 27–Aug 16 · 8×

    135M people above 35C; UK year-high 38.1C

    Recovery from heat happens at night. The fourth European heatwave of the summer pushed above 35C across France, Italy, Spain and the UK, with a UK year-high of 38.1C in west London and parts of France past 42C. More than 135 million people were forecast above 35C. June and July in western Europe were the hottest on record, averaging 21.62C - about 2.79C above normal. The debt is cumulative: the fourth wave lands on a body that never finished paying off the third.

    Why is the overnight low a better predictor of who dies in a heatwave than the afternoon high everyone actually talks about?
