  • Venus never got to keep a moon long enough for anyone to see it Sep 13–Sep 16 · 2×

    recent research finding University of California, Riverside

    New modeling from UC Riverside's Stephen Kane finds that almost any moon Venus once had would have spiraled inward and been torn apart by the planet's own gravity within about a billion years - explaining why, unlike Earth and Mars, Venus has none today.

    What does it mean for a planet to be built in a way that guarantees it destroys its own moon?

    Sep 16 UC Riverside puts a number on it: a moon half our Moon's mass would survive ~1.7 billion years before Venus's rotation pulled it apart; twice that mass, only ~30 million years. NASA's DAVINCI mission could look for atmospheric traces.

    1 earlier update
