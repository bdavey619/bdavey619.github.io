  • Lala never made landfall - and three days on, 117,000 customers are still dark Aug 16–Aug 18 · 4×

    88,670 still out statewide; Big Island 38,250 Hawaii Island

    At peak the storm took out 250,900 Hawaiian Electric customers - roughly one in four tracked accounts statewide - as the eyewall passed just south of Hawaii Island without an official landfall. By 6pm Monday that had fallen to about 88,670 still dark, with roughly 162,230 restored - progress, but unevenly distributed: the Big Island still had about 38,250 out, concentrated in Puna, North and South Kona, North and South Kohala, Hamakua and South Hilo, against about 14,350 on Oahu and 1,450 in Maui County. Gusts hit 91mph at Kohala, 89mph in West Maui and 84mph in elevated parts of Oahu, toppling trees onto poles and transmission towers. Hawaiian Electric's own estimate for the hardest-hit rural districts is still weeks - possibly months - even as the statewide total drops.

    Why is landfall still the headline metric for a storm when the damage is done by rain rate and terrain - and who is misled by that framing?

  • The Rhine went single-digit for the first time on record - and the number everyone is quoting isn't a depth Aug 16–Aug 18 · 3×

    Kaub 6cm; channel still ~1.2m; 18 of 34 landings out Rhine delta to lower Danube

    The Kaub gauge fell to 6cm late Friday and read 8cm early Saturday - the first single-digit reading since measurements began in the 19th century, against a previous record of 25cm set in 2018. One correction worth making: that gauge figure is a reading against a reference datum, not the depth of the water. The navigation channel at Kaub was still a little over 1.2 metres deep. It is simply far too shallow for laden barges and for most 110-135m river cruise ships, which is why 18 of 34 Viking landing stages between Amsterdam and Basel were inaccessible or unserved as of Aug 14, and why freight keeps shifting to road and rail. A barge that normally hauls 5,100 tonnes past Kaub is moving about 800, and Rotterdam freight to points south has reached roughly EUR 150 a tonne against EUR 20 in normal water. Rain is finally forecast for the Swiss and Alpine catchments and Kaub may recover modestly later in the week - but the soil is dry enough that isolated storms run off without recharging the river.

    When a number becomes the headline for a crisis, how often is it measuring the thing we think it is - and who benefits from the confusion?

Human Rituals

  • Back-to-school - a record total, and everyone shopping at the same two places Jul 26–Aug 18 · 10×

    Walmart ~80%; dollar stores in the top ten

    The NRF projects $146.8 billion in 2026 back-to-school spending, an all-time high, and JLL has parents budgeting $489 per child, up 11.7%. The composition is the story. Walmart's share of parents' shopping destinations climbed more than 22 percentage points in a year to nearly 80% - the highest any retailer has reached in the history of JLL's survey. Dollar stores entered the top ten for the first time, up about 10 points to nearly 32%. Mass merchandisers now reach more than 83% of back-to-school shoppers. The ritual is getting more expensive to perform and the places it is performed in are consolidating around price.

    What does it mean that the most reliable annual measure of household optimism is now rising for the same reason household strain is?

  • Omis was evacuated by boat - and the Adriatic fire wave now has four people in custody Aug 17–Aug 18 · 3×

    charges pending; terrorism angle under review Omis, Dalmatia, Croatia

    The fire out of Lokva Rogoznica covered the roughly 6km to Omis in hours, reaching the Borak neighbourhood above the town centre around 1:45am. Roughly 1,200 residents and tourists were moved out - by land and by sea, with harbour authority vessels lifting people from stretches the road could not reach, and some going into the water off boats to escape the heat. One woman died; about 40 were injured with ten in intensive care. Croatia's fire chief called it among the worst blazes in the country's history. Further up the same coast the story turned criminal: police arrested four people - three Croatians aged 42, 45 and 46 and a 33-year-old Polish citizen - shortly after 8pm on Aug 15, suspected of deliberately setting fires at ten locations across Zadar County that day and the day before, acting, investigators say, under a prior plan and mutual agreement. Officers seized a semi-automatic rifle with 44 rounds, a pistol and several mobile devices. The prosecutor's office is preparing formal charges, and Croatian media report USKOK is examining whether the arson contains elements of terrorism - no such charge has been confirmed publicly. A local couple who saw them lighting a fire near Rastevic took down the vehicle and called it in.

    What happens to a place whose geography is its whole economy when that same geography becomes the reason there is only one way out - and when the fire turns out to have been set?

Curiosities

  • An invasive tree is now the pacing item on Hawaii's power restoration Aug 17–Aug 18 · 3×

    Big Island still 38,250 out vs Oahu's 14,350 Hamakua coast, Hawaii Island

    The reason parts of Hawaii island may wait months rather than days for power is not the storm and not the poles - it is the trees. Many of the island's transmission lines run through wooded ground where albizia, a fast-growing invasive introduced for reforestation, is tall and brittle enough to take a line out when it goes over. Gusts of 91mph at Kohala put trees onto poles and transmission towers, and Hawaiian Electric said Monday that assessments found major damage to infrastructure in remote areas; crews are working a 42-mile transmission span on the Hamakua coast. Two days into restoration the pattern is holding exactly as the biology would predict: Oahu, with its more accessible urban circuits, is down to about 14,350 customers out, while the Big Island's wooded Puna-to-Hamakua corridor still had roughly 38,250 dark as of Monday night. The restoration schedule is being set by what grew alongside the corridor over decades, not by what the wind did in a night.

    How many of the systems we call infrastructure are actually being governed by something biological that nobody put on the asset register?

  • The 30-year hit 5.31% - a level last seen when 'subprime' was a new word Aug 8–Aug 18 · 8×

    30yr above 5.3%, 10yr near 4.75%

    The story moved to the long end of the curve. The 30-year yield topped 5.31% on Monday, its highest since July 2007 - the last time it was here, the iPhone had just gone on sale - and Tuesday it was still holding above 5.3%. Now the 10-year is no longer the quiet one either: it pushed toward 4.75%, up roughly 5 basis points, dragging the Nasdaq down 1.02% while the S&P 500 fell 0.44% and the Dow 0.21%, with tech names hit hardest by the rise in long-dated borrowing costs. Three things are pushing the long end: the Treasury is issuing an enormous volume of debt to fund persistent deficits, and more supply means worse prices; tariffs and rising energy costs have kept inflation alive past the point the Fed expected it to fade; and the market is still recalibrating around Kevin Warsh, who took the chair in May and whose signals on the path of rates have introduced fresh uncertainty.

    When the long bond reprices this far, is the market forecasting inflation - or repricing the credibility of the borrower?

  • The 60-day clock ran out, and Brent settled at $90.87 Aug 8–Aug 18 · 8×

    fresh vessel strike; Brent ~$91, oil highest since July

    The memorandum Trump and Pezeshkian signed on June 17 - which declared the removal of the US naval blockade and arrangements for safe passage of commercial vessels, explicitly 'for 60 days only' - expired Monday with no successor, and Tuesday brought the reason a successor hasn't arrived: a fresh strike was reported on a vessel transiting the strait, pushing oil to its highest level since July, with Brent near $91 and WTI around $84-85. Iran said Tuesday the strait will stay shut until the US lifts sanctions and the blockade - not a negotiating position, a condition. Trump, in the same 24 hours, threatened for a second time to 'bomb the shit' out of Oman if it 'gets in the way' of a deal, even as Oman is the country actually hosting the talks Iran says it's close to concluding with Muscat, not Washington. Before Feb 28 roughly a fifth of the world's oil and gas moved through the strait. The premium was never pricing the negotiation; it was pricing what happens when the negotiation has an expiry date printed on it - and now a second one, on the mediator's patience.

    At what point does a war premium stop being a risk premium and simply become the price?

  • 171 days of war, an expired agreement, and still no congressional vote Jul 22–Aug 18 · 21×

    day 172; second Oman threat, no vote

    The one document that structured this war expired Monday, and Tuesday made the vacuum concrete. The June 17 memorandum ran 60 days by its own terms; both sides had accused the other of violating it for weeks, and neither moved to extend. Iran said Tuesday the strait stays shut until the US lifts sanctions and the blockade - a condition, not an opening position - while a fresh strike was reported on a vessel transiting the strait. Trump responded not with Iran but with Oman: for the second time, he threatened to 'bomb the shit' out of the country actually hosting the talks Tehran says it's close to concluding. Control of the strait - about a fifth of world oil and gas before Feb 28 - remains the sticking point. What still has not happened, on the second day without a governing agreement: any congressional vote. The War Powers clock was first paused by a ceasefire, then never started by a blockade, and has now outlasted the agreement that replaced both.

    How does a country fight a 171-day war that closes the world's most important oil lane without Congress ever casting a vote - and what mechanism was supposed to prevent that?

  • The fire front reached Belgium - and is now approaching the German border Jul 26–Aug 18 · 14×

    High Fens ~3,000ha, largest in Belgian history

    The High Fens fire in Belgium's Liege province, which broke out Friday, more than doubled in 24 hours to about 3,000 hectares and is now the largest wildfire in Belgium's recorded history - and it had been advancing on the German border closely enough that Monschau, on the German side, was put under an evacuation order. Rain over eastern Belgium on Monday morning helped: officials say the fire is no longer advancing toward Germany, though a crisis-cell spokesman was explicit that 'we cannot yet say it is contained' and it 'could change according to the weather.' Some 500 firefighters from Belgium, Germany and Luxembourg are on it, plus three helicopters and two water-bombing aircraft brought in under the EU Civil Protection Mechanism, activated Saturday. France's major blaze was stabilised the same day; in Spain a soldier died fighting a major fire in Aragon; in Portugal five firefighters were hurt when their vehicle caught fire near Dine; Greece is investigating a deadly blaze of its own. Across Europe this year, fires have killed more than 20 people and burned roughly 576,000 hectares - twice the size of Luxembourg, and about 2.3x the 20-year average for the date, though still below 2025's pace.

    If the fire season is one continuous system moving across terrain rather than a sequence of separate fires, what decides where it goes next?

  • The tsunami warning cleared in hours; by Independence Day the toll was 68 and 12,800 had lost their homes Aug 16–Aug 18 · 3×

    toll 68; 12,800 displaced Flores, East Nusa Tenggara, Indonesia

    The M7.7 hit off Flores at 5:58am on Aug 15. Indonesia issued a tsunami warning and lifted it the same morning when monitoring showed no significant sea-level change; the waves that came were under 1.6m. That was the fast, correct, quantitative answer. The other number took three days: 2, then 20, 38, 47, 51, 53, and 68 by Monday, with displacement climbing to 12,800. Landslides are still blocking sections of the Trans-Flores Highway. Monday was Aug 17 - Indonesia's Independence Day - which the country marked with a fifth of a province in shelters. Officials say some residents will not go back inside because of the aftershocks and the memory of the 1992 Flores quake and tsunami.

    Why does a tsunami warning resolve in hours while the death toll takes days - and what does that asymmetry say about which disasters we have learned to measure?
